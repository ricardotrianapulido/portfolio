01 Executive Summary

A fast, research-driven product design process for launching a competitive credit card insurance experience.

Context

Itaú Bank needed to launch a protected credit card insurance product and compete in the digital insurance market.

Challenge

The product had to be designed, aligned, prototyped, and validated within one sprint without sacrificing clarity or trust.

My Role

I led UX strategy, research, benchmarking, wireframing, prototyping, validation, and stakeholder alignment.

Outcome

The solution improved conversion, achieved 100% task success, and contributed reusable patterns to the Design System.

The Challenge

A one-sprint launch with no room for weak product clarity.

The team needed to design a digital insurance experience quickly, while making the product easy to understand, trustworthy, and competitive against established assisted sales models.

01. Aggressive timeline (Full UX process in one sprint)

Leadership Decisions

Decisions that moved the product forward.

Navigating a tight one-sprint deadline meant every design decision had to be strategic, focusing on the highest-impact improvements for trust and conversion.

Focus

Prioritize clarity over speed alone

Despite the sprint rush, we refused to skip research. A fast benchmark ensured we didn't launch an inferior experience.

Trade-offSpent more time on copywriting to reduce support calls.
Trust

Make benefits visible early

Moving benefit details from the plan screen to the landing page increased confidence before the buy intent.

Trade-offSimplified the technical plan comparison.
Competitive

Use benchmark to define competitiveness

Directly compared our digital offer against the market leader's assisted sales flow to identify gaps.

Trade-offAdopted aggressive visual cues for unique selling points.
System

Contribute reusable patterns

The insurance intro component was built to be reused for other banking insurance products, saving future time.

Trade-offLimited custom UI to stick to global DS tokens.
05 Evidence & Key Insights
01
Assisted sales set higher trust

The digital experience needed to match the human touch of telephone sales in explaining complex terms.

02
Legal terms created gaps

Users felt hesitant when legal clauses were presented as blocks of text without context or simple language.

03
Benefits needed to feel tangible

Static lists were ignored; users responded better when benefits were tied to specific credit card use cases.
